# 🎎 Cultural Etiquette & Manners

**Golden Rule:** Be quiet 🤫, be clean ✨, be aware of your surroundings 👀.

**For First-Timers:** Japanese culture values harmony (和 wa), consideration for others, and cleanliness. Following these guidelines shows respect and helps you blend in. Don't stress too much - locals are understanding of tourists, but effort is appreciated!

## 🚄 Trains & Transit

### On Trains:
- **Silence:** 🔇 **No phone calls.** Set phone to silent/vibrate. If you must take a call, get off at next station.
  - Texting is OK; talking quietly with travel companion is OK
  - Background music from headphones should be inaudible to others
- **Priority Seats (優先席):** 
  - Usually at train ends; marked in different color (often blue/purple)
  - For elderly, pregnant, disabled, or those with small children
  - If you sit and someone who needs it enters, **immediately stand up and offer seat**
  - Some young Japanese avoid these seats entirely to be safe
- **Backpacks:** 🎒 
  - **Crowded trains:** Remove backpack and hold at your feet or on overhead rack
  - Wear on front (chest) if keeping it on
  - You're taking up more space than you think!
- **Eating:** 🍱
  - ✅ **OK:** Shinkansen (bullet trains), long-distance express trains
  - ❌ **NOT OK:** Subways, local trains, buses
  - Exception: Water is always OK
  - **Why:** Smells can bother others in confined spaces
- **Standing Position:**
  - Face the door or stand parallel to train movement
  - Don't block doors when train is stopping
  - Let passengers OFF before boarding ON
- **Rush Hour (7-9 AM, 5-7 PM):**
  - Trains PACKED (people pushers exist!)
  - Avoid if possible; if not, go with the flow
  - Remove backpack; hold bags close to body
- **Luggage:**
  - Large suitcases: Use overhead racks or stand near doors (not blocking)
  - Shinkansen has space behind last row seats (best spot for big bags)

### At Stations:
- **Escalators:**
  - **Tokyo & most of Japan:** Stand LEFT ⬅️, Walk RIGHT
  - **Osaka (and Kyoto sometimes):** Stand RIGHT ➡️, Walk LEFT
  - **If unsure:** Watch what locals do and follow
- **Lines:** Queue in an orderly fashion; don't cut
- **Platform:** Stand behind yellow line when waiting; don't lean over edge

## 🥢 Dining

### Chopstick Rules:
- ❌ **Never:** Stick chopsticks vertically in rice (仏箸 hotoke bashi) - funeral ritual for dead ⚰️
- ❌ **Never:** Pass food chopstick-to-chopstick (箸渡し hashi watashi) - funeral ritual (bone passing)
- ❌ **Never:** Cross chopsticks on your plate (looks like "X" for death)
- ❌ **Never:** Wave chopsticks around while talking or point at people
- ✅ **Do:** Use the chopstick rest (箸置き hashi oki) when not actively eating
- ✅ **Do:** Turn chopsticks around (use blunt end) when taking food from shared plates
- ✅ **Alternative:** Use serving chopsticks/spoon if provided

### Table Manners:
- **Slurping:** 🍜
  - ✅ **Encouraged** for ramen, soba, udon - shows enjoyment + cools noodles
  - Loud slurping is totally normal (don't be shy!)
  - **But:** Don't slurp rice or soup
- **Tipping:** 💴
  - ❌ **DO NOT TIP. EVER.**
  - Not expected, not wanted, will confuse/offend staff
  - The price on the menu is the price you pay
  - Exception: Some high-end hotels/tours with Westerners may accept (but still not required)
- **"Itadakimasu" & "Gochisousama":**
  - **Before eating:** Say "Itadakimasu" (いただきます) = "I humbly receive"
    - Hands together in prayer gesture (optional but nice)
  - **After eating:** Say "Gochisousama deshita" (ごちそうさまでした) = "It was a feast"
    - Say to staff when leaving
- **Oshibori (お絞り):**
  - Hot/cold wet towel provided at start of meal
  - Use to wipe HANDS only (not face, not neck, just hands)
  - Fold neatly and place on table when done
- **Payment:**
  - Bring bill/check to register (don't pay at table, except high-end places)
  - Cash tray: Place money in small tray, not directly in hand
  - Change returned in same tray
- **Shoes Off:**
  - If you step UP to enter restaurant = remove shoes
  - Slippers usually provided
  - Keep socks clean (holes = embarrassing!)
- **Shared Plates:**
  - Family-style eating common (ramen/donburi usually individual)
  - Use serving utensils or reverse your chopsticks
- **Finish Your Food:**
  - Leaving food can be seen as wasteful (but not a major offense)
  - If you can't finish, say "Gochisousama" and apologize politely
- **Drinking:**
  - Don't pour your own drink (beer/sake) - pour for others, they'll pour for you
  - Hold cup with both hands when someone pours for you (respect)
  - "Kanpai!" (乾杯) = "Cheers!" before first sip

## 🗑️ Trash & Cleanliness

- **No Public Bins:** 🚮
  - **Why:** Removed after 1995 Tokyo subway attack (security)
  - You will carry trash ALL DAY
  - Bring a small plastic bag 🛍️ in your backpack/purse
- **Where to Find Bins:**
  - ✅ **Convenience stores** (Konbini: 7-Eleven, FamilyMart, Lawson) - bins inside for customers
  - ✅ **Train stations** - usually near ticket gates (for station-purchased items)
  - ✅ **Vending machines** - recycling bins next to them for bottles/cans ONLY 🥤
  - ✅ **Parks** - some have bins near entrances
  - ✅ **Your hotel room** - save trash til evening
- **Recycling:**
  - Bins separated: Plastic, burnable, cans, bottles, paper
  - If unsure, watch locals or ask
- **Don't Litter:**
  - Extremely frowned upon; you'll stand out as disrespectful tourist
  - Japanese keep cities clean through personal responsibility

## 🚶 Walking & Public Behavior

- **No Eating & Walking:** 🍦
  - ✅ **OK:** Theme parks, festivals, some shopping streets
  - ❌ **Avoid:** Residential areas, business districts, trains/buses
  - **Why:** Seen as sloppy; you might drop food/trash
  - **Solution:** Eat at shop, find bench, or stand still while eating
- **Escalators (Repeated for emphasis):**
  - **Tokyo:** Stand Left ⬅️, Walk Right
  - **Osaka:** Stand Right ➡️, Walk Left
- **Smoking:** 🚬
  - **Street smoking:** BANNED in most cities (¥1,000-2,000 fine if caught)
  - **Smoke ONLY in:**
    - Designated smoking areas (usually near stations, marked with signs)
    - Smoking rooms in restaurants (ask "Kitsuen-seki arimasu ka?" 喫煙席ありますか)
  - Vaping follows same rules as smoking
  - Don't throw cigarette butts on ground (portable ashtrays sold at konbini)
- **Volume:**
  - Keep voice down in public (trains, restaurants, streets)
  - Loud talking = rude American tourist stereotype
  - Laugh quietly
- **Personal Space:**
  - Don't hug/kiss in public (very rare in Japan)
  - Handshakes rare (bow is standard greeting)
  - Keep distance when talking to strangers
- **Photos:**
  - Don't photograph strangers without permission
  - Especially: Geisha/Maiko (they're working, not exhibits)
  - Respect "No Photography" signs (common in temples, museums, shops)
- **Queuing:**
  - Line up patiently; don't cut
  - If unsure where line starts, ask "Saigo no hito wa doko desu ka?" (最後の人はどこですか - "Where is the last person?")
- **Blowing Nose:**
  - Don't blow nose in public (seen as gross)
  - Sniffling is more acceptable (weird, but true)
  - Go to restroom if you need to blow nose loudly

## ⛩️ Temples & Shrines

### Entering:
- **Torii Gates (⛩️):**
  - Bow once BEFORE passing under gate (entering sacred space)
  - Walk on SIDES of path (center is for spirits/kami)
  - Bow again when LEAVING
- **Main Buildings:**
  - Remove shoes if required (slippers often provided)
  - No shoes on tatami mats
- **Silence:**
  - Quiet, contemplative atmosphere
  - Whisper if you must talk

### Purification Ritual (Temizuya 手水舎):
**Before approaching main hall, purify at water basin:**
1. Pick up ladle with RIGHT hand
2. Pour water over LEFT hand
3. Switch ladle to LEFT hand
4. Pour water over RIGHT hand
5. Switch back to RIGHT hand
6. Pour water into LEFT palm
7. Rinse MOUTH with that water (don't drink directly from ladle!)
8. Spit water beside basin (NOT back into it)
9. Rinse LEFT hand again
10. Hold ladle vertical to rinse handle
11. Place ladle face down on basin
- **Note:** Don't touch ladle to lips; use your hand as cup

### Prayer/Worship:
**At Shrine (神社 jinja - Shinto):**
1. Toss coin into offering box (¥5 is good luck - "go-en" 御縁 = connection)
2. Bow twice (2 deep bows)
3. Clap twice (2 claps, hands together)
4. Make wish/pray silently
5. Bow once (1 deep bow)
- **Mnemonic:** "Ni-rei, ni-hakushu, ichi-rei" (2 bows, 2 claps, 1 bow)

**At Temple (お寺 otera - Buddhist):**
- No clapping (that's Shinto!)
- Just bow, toss coin, pray silently, bow again
- May ring bell before praying (if available)
- Incense offering: Light incense, wave smoke toward yourself (purification)

### Photography:
- ✅ **Usually OK:** Temple/shrine grounds, gardens, exterior buildings
- ❌ **Usually NOT OK:** Inside main halls, near Buddha statues, during ceremonies
- **Look for signs:** 📷❌ or 撮影禁止 (satsuei kinshi = photography prohibited)
- **When in doubt:** Ask staff or follow what locals are doing

### Other Tips:
- Don't point at Buddha/deity statues (use open palm if gesturing)
- Omikuji (fortune papers): If bad fortune, tie it to rack at shrine (leaves bad luck there)
- Ema (wooden wishes): Write wish, hang on rack
- Omamori (charms): OK to buy as souvenirs; traditionally kept in wallet/bag for luck

## ♨️ Onsen / Public Baths (If You Visit)

### Before Entering Bath:
- **Wash First:** 🚿
  - Sit on small stool at washing stations
  - Soap + rinse COMPLETELY before entering communal bath
  - Scrub every part of body
  - This is NON-NEGOTIABLE
- **No Swimsuits:** 👙❌
  - Must be completely naked (gender-separated baths)
  - Small towel (tenugui) provided: Use to cover private areas while walking, leave outside bath or fold on head
- **Hair:** 💇‍♀️
  - Tie long hair up (hair tie or clip)
  - Hair must NOT touch bath water

### Tattoos: 🐉
- **Problem:** Associated with yakuza (organized crime)
- **Reality:** Many onsens BAN tattoos (yes, even small Western tattoos)
- **Solutions:**
  - **Private onsen:** Book private family bath (kashikiri, 貸切) - usually ¥2,000-5,000 for 45 min
  - **Tattoo-friendly onsens:** Research online (tattoo-friendly.jp)
  - **Tattoo cover stickers:** Sold at Donki, some onsens (only works for small tattoos)
  - **Hotel onsen:** Private in-room baths (high-end hotels)
- **Don't:** Try to sneak in with tattoos if banned (you'll be asked to leave, awkward)

### In the Bath:
- Enter slowly (water is HOT: 38-42°C/100-108°F)
- No swimming, splashing, or playing
- Small towel stays OUT of water (on head, on side of bath, or in locker)
- Sit still; enjoy soaking
- Quiet conversation OK; loud talking NOT OK
- Stay hydrated (drink water before/after)

### General Onsen Etiquette:
- No photos/phones in bathing area (locker room or baths)
- Don't stare at others
- No shaving in washing area
- Return all items (stool, bucket) to where you found them
- Dry off with small towel BEFORE entering changing room (don't drip everywhere)

## 🙇 General Respect & Manners

- **Bowing:**
  - Casual: 15° head nod
  - Normal: 30° bow from waist
  - Deep respect: 45° bow (meeting important person, deep apology)
  - When receiving service: Small nod is fine
- **Shoes:**
  - Remove when: Entering homes, some restaurants, temples, ryokan, dressing rooms
  - Line shoes up pointing toward exit (makes leaving easier)
  - Don't step on the threshold (frame between inside/outside)
- **Cash Culture:**
  - Credit cards increasingly accepted, but cash is king
  - Always have ¥10,000+ on you
  - Small shops, temples, street food = cash only
- **Punctuality:**
  - Be on time (Japanese trains to the second!)
  - If late for reservation, call ahead
- **Business Cards:**
  - If given business card (名刺 meishi), receive with both hands, read it, thank person, place carefully in wallet (don't shove in pocket)
- **Apologies:**
  - "Sumimasen" (すみません) = Excuse me / Sorry / Thank you (multipurpose word!)
  - Say it a lot: Bumping into someone, getting attention, ordering, receiving help
- **Masks:**
  - Some Japanese wear masks when sick (consideration for others)
  - If you have a cold, wearing a mask is polite
  - Post-COVID, masks less common but still accepted

## ❗ Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. ❌ Talking loudly on train
2. ❌ Tipping at restaurant
3. ❌ Wearing shoes on tatami mats
4. ❌ Sticking chopsticks in rice vertically
5. ❌ Not washing before entering onsen
6. ❌ Eating while walking in business districts
7. ❌ Smoking on the street
8. ❌ Littering or leaving trash
9. ❌ Taking photos of geisha without permission
10. ❌ Blocking train doors / not moving into train interior
11. ❌ Not standing aside on escalator
12. ❌ Blowing nose loudly at restaurant table
13. ❌ Entering temple without purification ritual
14. ❌ Touching shrine/temple offerings without permission
15. ❌ Assuming everyone speaks English (learn a few phrases!)

## ✅ When in Doubt...

1. **Watch what locals do** and follow their lead
2. **Ask politely:** "Sumimasen, kore wa ii desu ka?" (すみません、これはいいですか) = "Excuse me, is this OK?"
3. **Apologize if you make a mistake:** "Sumimasen" + bow
4. **Smile and be respectful:** Goes a long way even if you mess up
5. **Learn basic phrases:** Shows effort and respect (see survival-japanese.md)

**Remember:** You're a guest in Japan. Show respect for the culture, and you'll have an amazing experience! Most Japanese are extremely forgiving of tourists who try their best. 🇯🇵
