# English Speaker's Essential Guide to Japan

**Target Audience:** English-only speakers visiting Japan for the first time.

This guide covers everything non-Japanese speakers need to know to navigate Japan confidently,
from language barriers to payment methods and cultural nuances.

### 📸 Using Google Translate Camera (Step-by-Step)

1. **Open Google Translate app** → Tap "Camera" icon
2. **Select Languages:** English ↔ Japanese
3. **Point camera at text:** Menu, sign, instruction label
4. **Result:** Text is replaced in real-time with English translation
5. **Tip:** Tap "Pause" to freeze translation, then screenshot for reference

**Best Use Cases:**

- Restaurant menus (especially izakayas with handwritten specials)
- Vending machine buttons
- Laundromat instructions
- Store return policies
- Train station warning signs

**Limitations:**

- Handwriting recognition is spotty (printed text works better)
- Food names may translate literally ("Iron Pot Chicken" instead of "Yakitori")

### "English Friendly" Scale (Used Throughout This Guide)

- **High:** Staff speak conversational English, English menus/signs available.
- **Medium:** Staff speak basic English phrases ("How many?", "This one?"), picture menus.
- **Low:** Little to no English. Pointing, gestures, and translation apps required.

## 💳 Payment Methods: Cash vs. Card

**CRITICAL:** Japan is **STILL cash-heavy** despite modernization efforts. Always carry ¥10,000-20,000 ($70-130) in cash.

### Where to Use Credit Cards

| Place Type | Accepts Cards? | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Hotels** | ✅ Always | Prefer contactless/Apple Pay at check-in. |
| **Department Stores** | ✅ Always | Bic Camera, Uniqlo, Don Quijote, Muji, etc. |
| **Chain Restaurants** | ✅ Usually | Look for Visa/Mastercard stickers at entrance. |
| **Convenience Stores** | ✅ Always | 7-Eleven, Lawson, FamilyMart accept IC cards (Suica) and credit cards. |
| **Small Restaurants** | ❌ Rarely | Cash only. ATM nearby or pay at hotel later (if arranged). |
| **Ramen Shops** | ❌ Usually | Ticket vending machines require cash (some new machines accept IC cards). |
| **Street Vendors** | ❌ Never | Cash only. |
| **Temples/Shrines** | ❌ Cash only | Offerings, fortune slips (omikuji), and charms (omamori) are cash-based. |

### Best Credit Cards for Japan

**Recommended (High Acceptance):**

- **Visa:** 90%+ acceptance rate
- **Mastercard:** 85%+ acceptance rate
- **American Express:** 60% acceptance (mostly chains, tourist areas)

**Avoid:**

- **Discover:** Almost never accepted outside of tourist hotels

### ATM Strategy

**Where to Find English-Language ATMs:**

| Location | ATM Type | Hours | Fees |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **7-Eleven** | Seven Bank | 24/7 | ¥110 ($0.75) + your bank's fee |
| **Lawson** | Lawson Bank | 24/7 | ¥110 ($0.75) + your bank's fee |
| **Japan Post Bank** | JP Bank | 9 AM - 9 PM (weekdays)<br>9 AM - 5 PM (weekends) | ¥110 ($0.75) + your bank's fee |
| **Airport (Narita/Haneda)** | Various | 24/7 | Higher fees (~¥300-500 + bank fees) |

**How to Use a 7-Eleven ATM:**

1. Insert your international debit/credit card
2. Select **"English"** (usually top-right button)
3. Select **"Withdrawal"** (not "Balance Inquiry" - it may fail)
4. Enter amount: ¥10,000 / ¥20,000 / ¥30,000
5. Confirm fees
6. Take cash + receipt

**Tips:**

- **Withdraw large amounts** to minimize ATM fees (max: ¥50,000-100,000 per transaction)
- **Notify your bank** before travel (fraud prevention)
- **Use debit cards** (not credit cards) to avoid cash advance fees
- **Best Cards for Travel:** Charles Schwab (refunds all ATM fees), Ally Bank (no foreign transaction fees)

## 🚇 Navigation & Transportation

### Reading Train Station Signs

**Sign Hierarchy (Top to Bottom):**

1. **Station Name (Kanji):** 東京 (Tokyo), 京都 (Kyoto)
2. **Station Name (Romanized English):** "Tokyo", "Kyoto"
3. **Line Name + Station Number:** "JR Yamanote Line" / "Y-01"
4. **Platform Number:** "Track 3" / "3番線" (Sanbansen)

**Example:**

```text
東京駅          ← Kanji (ignore if you can't read)
Tokyo Station    ← English name (focus here)
JR Yamanote Line ← Line name
Track 5          ← Platform (follow this!)
```

### Exit Strategy: Why Exit Numbers Matter

**Tokyo/Kyoto/Osaka stations have 20-30 exits.** Picking the wrong exit = 10-minute detour.

**How to Use Exit Numbers:**

1. **Google Maps** will say: "Exit C4" or "Ginza Line Exit 3"
2. **At the station:** Look for **hanging signs** (yellow background, blue letters)
   - Signs say: "Exit C4 →"
3. **Follow arrows** until you see daylight

**Pro Tip:** Screenshot Google Maps directions **before** entering the station. Reception is spotty underground.

**Common Exit Mistakes:**

- ❌ "I'll figure out the exit when I get there." *(You won't. The station is a maze.)*
- ❌ Asking staff for help without the exit number. *(They'll point to the nearest exit, not your destination.)*
- ✅ **Memorize:** "Exit C4" + screenshot = Success

### IC Card (Suica/PASMO) Usage

**What Is It?**

A rechargeable transit card that works on **all trains, subways, and buses** nationwide.

**Setup (Mobile Suica in Apple Wallet):**

1. Open **Apple Wallet** → Tap **"+"**
2. Select **"Transit Card"** → **"Suica"**
3. Add ¥1,000-2,000 to start
4. **Top Up:** Wallet app → Select Suica → "Add Money" (use Mastercard or Amex, NOT Visa)

**How to Use:**

- **Enter Gate:** Tap phone on yellow reader
- **Exit Gate:** Tap phone on yellow reader again (fare is auto-deducted)
- **If Card Fails:** Gate closes. Tap again or ask station staff ("Sumimasen...")

**Non-Train Uses:**

- **Convenience Stores:** 7-Eleven, Lawson, FamilyMart
- **Vending Machines:** Look for IC card logo
- **Lockers:** Coin lockers at stations (some accept Suica)

**Common Mistakes:**

- ❌ Forgetting to tap out (you'll be charged maximum fare)
- ❌ Using Visa to top up Mobile Suica (fails 90% of time for foreign cards)

## 🍜 Dining Without Japanese

### Restaurant Types & English-Friendliness

| Type | English Level | Ordering Method | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Chain Restaurants** | Medium | Picture menus or tablet ordering | Saizeriya, Coco Ichibanya, Yoshinoya, Sukiya. |
| **Department Store Restaurants** | Medium-High | Picture menus, English on some tablets | 10th-11th floors usually have food courts. |
| **Izakayas (Casual)** | Low | Point at menu pictures or other tables' food | Say "Kore" (This) and point. |
| **Ramen Shops** | Low | Ticket vending machine (cash) | Use Google Translate camera on buttons. Most popular = top-left button. |
| **Hotel Restaurants** | High | English menus, English-speaking staff | Pricier but stress-free. |
| **Conveyor Belt Sushi** | Medium | Tablet ordering (English option) | Kura Sushi, Sushiro have full English menus on tablets. |

### Decoding Vending Machine Menus (Ramen/Gyudon Shops)

**Step-by-Step:**

1. **Look at Pictures:** Most buttons have small food photos
2. **Top-Left Button:** Usually the most popular dish (chef's recommendation)
3. **Insert Cash:** ¥1,000 bills accepted
4. **Press Button:** Ticket prints out
5. **Hand Ticket to Staff:** They'll prepare your order

**Common Buttons (with Kanji/English):**

| Button Label | Meaning | Translation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **大盛り** | *Ōmori* | Large portion (+¥100-200) |
| **味玉** | *Ajitama* | Marinated soft-boiled egg (+¥100) |
| **替え玉** | *Kaedama* | Extra noodles (after you finish first bowl) |
| **白米** | *Hakumai* | White rice (side dish, ~¥150) |

**Tip:** If confused, **point at the ticket machine and say "Osusume wa?"** (What's recommended?).
Staff will point to a button.

### Menu Survival Phrases

| Situation | Japanese | Pronunciation | When to Use |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Entering Restaurant** | いらっしゃいませ | *Irasshaimase* | Staff greets you (no need to respond) |
| **"Table for 2"** | 二人です | *Futari desu* | Hold up 2 fingers while saying this |
| **"Recommendation?"** | おすすめは？ | *Osusume wa?* | When staring at menu, completely lost |
| **"I can't eat [X]"** | [X]が食べられません | *[X] ga taberaremasen* | Allergies: "Shellfish ga taberaremasen" |
| **"Check, please"** | お会計 | *O-kaikei* | Say this when leaving (don't expect a bill brought to table) |
| **"Thank you (leaving)"** | ごちそうさまでした | *Gochisousama deshita* | Said to staff as you exit |

**Critical Note:** **Most restaurants do NOT bring the check to your table.** You walk to the register
near the exit and pay there.

## 🛍️ Shopping & Retail Phrases

### At the Register

**What Cashiers Will Ask (with Answers):**

| Japanese | Pronunciation | Meaning | Your Response |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **袋は要りますか？** | *Fukuro wa irimasu ka?* | Need a bag? (~¥5) | "Hai" (Yes) / "Daijoubu desu" (No thanks) |
| **ポイントカードは？** | *Pointo kaado wa?* | Do you have a points card? | "Arimasen" (No) or just shake head |
| **レシートは？** | *Reshiito wa?* | Do you want receipt? | "Hai" (Yes - keep for tax-free) |
| **一括で？** | *Ikkatsu de?* | Pay all at once? | "Hai" (Yes) |

**Tax-Free Shopping:**

- **Minimum Purchase:** ¥5,000 ($33) at same store on same day
- **Required:** Passport (carry it when shopping)
- **How:** Pay normally, then go to "Tax-Free Counter" with receipt + passport
  - Staff staples a form into your passport (don't remove until leaving Japan)
- **Savings:** 10% refund (worth it on electronics, cosmetics, clothing)
- [Full Tax-Free Guide](../Reference/tax-free-shopping.md)

## 🏨 Hotels & Accommodation

### What to Expect (Western vs. Japanese Differences)

| Feature | Japan | US/Europe |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Room Size** | Tiny (150-200 sq ft) | Larger (300-400 sq ft) |
| **Bed Size** | "Double" = ~55" wide (full-size) | "Double" = 60" (queen-size) |
| **Check-in Time** | 3:00 PM (strict) | Flexible early check-in |
| **Luggage Hold** | Always available (before check-in) | Sometimes available |
| **Slippers** | Provided (wear inside room) | Not provided |
| **Bathroom** | Shower/Tub separated, toilet separate (sometimes) | Combined bathroom |
| **Toiletries** | Full set (shampoo, conditioner, razor, toothbrush) | Basic only |

### Common Hotel Requests (English-Friendly)

| Request | How to Ask |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Early Luggage Drop-Off** | "Can I leave my luggage here?" (Reception will say yes) |
| **Late Check-Out** | "Is late check-out available?" (Often ¥1,000-2,000/hour) |
| **Extra Towels** | "Can I have extra towels?" (Call front desk or ask at reception) |
| **Hot Water Kettle** | Usually in room. If not: "Denki pot arimasu ka?" |

| **Laundry Service** | "Laundry service?" (Expensive - use coin laundromat instead) |

**Pro Tip:** If staying at a **business hotel** (Toyoko Inn, APA Hotel, Dormy Inn), expect:

- ✅ Small but efficient rooms (140-180 sq ft)
- ✅ Always clean, Western beds, free breakfast (sometimes)
- ✅ No frills but reliable
- ❌ Limited English (picture menus at check-in work fine)

## 🚨 Emergencies & Safety

### Emergency Numbers (English Available)

| Number | Service | English Support? | When to Use |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **110** | Police | Limited | Theft, lost passport, crime |
| **119** | Fire/Ambulance | Limited | Medical emergency, fire |
| **#9110** | Police Consultation | Limited | Non-emergency advice |
| **03-3501-0110** | Japan Helpline | Full English | 24/7 English support for any issue (lost items, medical, legal) |

**Japan Helpline (CRITICAL):**

- **Number:** 03-3501-0110 (Tokyo area code, works nationwide)
- **Service:** 24/7 English-speaking operators
- **Cost:** Free
- **Use For:** Any situation where language barrier is a problem
  - Lost passport → They call embassy for you
  - Medical issue → They call hospital and translate
  - Police report → They conference call with police
- [Official Site](https://jhelp.com/)

### Lost Items (Japan's Superpower)

**Japan has an ~80% lost item recovery rate.** Seriously.

**If You Lose Something:**

1. **On Train/Subway:**
   - Report at station office ("Wasuremono" - Lost Item)
   - Items held for 3-7 days at station
   - Then transferred to central Lost & Found

2. **In Taxi:**
   - Call taxi company (hotel staff can help)
   - Taxis have GPS logs - they'll know your route

3. **At Restaurant/Store:**
   - Return within 24 hours - usually still there
   - Staff hold lost items for weeks

**Central Lost & Found Centers:**

| City | Location | Hours | Contact |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Tokyo** | Tokyo Metropolitan Police Dept | 8:30 AM - 5:15 PM (weekdays) | 03-3814-4151 |
| **Kyoto** | Kyoto City Lost & Found | 9 AM - 5 PM (weekdays) | 075-256-3030 |
| **Osaka** | Osaka Prefectural Police | 9 AM - 5:45 PM (weekdays) | 06-6943-1234 |

**What You Need:**

- **Description:** Color, brand, where you lost it
- **Passport:** For identity verification
- **Someone to Translate:** Bring a hotel staff member or call Japan Helpline

## 📞 Staying Connected: Calling & Texting

### International Calling

#### Option 1: WiFi Calling (Best)

- **Setup:** Enable in iPhone Settings → Phone → WiFi Calling
- **Cost:** Free (uses hotel/cafe WiFi)
- **Limitations:** Only works with US carriers that support WiFi calling (AT&T, Verizon, T-Mobile)

#### Option 2: FaceTime Audio / WhatsApp Calls

- **Cost:** Free (internet-based)
- **Quality:** Good on WiFi, spotty on mobile data

#### Option 3: Google Voice

- **Cost:** $0.01-0.02/min to US numbers
- **Setup:** Install app before trip
- **Use:** Call US numbers over WiFi/data

### Calling Japanese Numbers (Hotels, Restaurants)

**From US Phone (Using Japan eSIM):**

- **Format:** Just dial the number as shown (no country code needed)
- **Example:** Restaurant shows "03-1234-5678" → Dial exactly that

**Common Prefixes:**

- **03:** Tokyo area
- **06:** Osaka area
- **075:** Kyoto area
- **050:** VoIP numbers (cheap, used by businesses)

**Tip:** If restaurant booking required, ask your hotel concierge to call for you.

## 🧳 Cultural Tips for English Speakers

### What Surprises First-Time Visitors

**Pleasant Surprises:**

- ✅ **Silence on trains** - No phone calls, minimal talking
- ✅ **No tipping** - Service charge included, tipping is offensive
- ✅ **Safety** - Leave laptop at cafe table, it'll be there when you return
- ✅ **Vending machines everywhere** - Hot coffee, cold beer, even hot soup

**Challenges:**

- ❌ **Coin usage** - You'll accumulate ¥1,000+ in coins ($7). Use them!
  - Convenience stores accept mountains of coins at self-checkout
- ❌ **Lack of trash cans** - Carry a small bag for trash (dispose at konbini)
- ❌ **Small portion sizes** - Order 2 dishes if hungry (normal in Japan)
- ❌ **No eating while walking** - Considered rude (except festival areas)

### Gestures & Non-Verbal Communication

| Gesture | Meaning | When to Use |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Bow (slight nod)** | Thank you / Sorry / Hello | When receiving help, entering shops, saying goodbye |
| **Crossed arms (X shape)** | No / Not available / Closed | Staff use this to indicate "sold out" or "wrong direction" |
| **Pointing at yourself (tap nose)** | Me? / I? | When staff asks "How many?" and you're alone |
| **Beckoning (palm down, fingers waving down)** | Come here | US-style "come here" (palm up) is rude in Japan |
| **Two fingers forming "L"** | Requesting a receipt | At konbini registers |

**Pro Tip:** **Bowing works for everything.** When in doubt, slight bow = universal politeness signal.
