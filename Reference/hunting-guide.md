#  Retro Gaming Hunt: The Ultimate Guide

**Target:** Famicom, Super Famicom, N64, GameBoy, Sega Saturn, Dreamcast.
**Golden Rule:** "If you see it and love it, buy it. You won't find it again."

##  The Big Three (City Centers)

### 1. Akihabara (Tokyo) - *The Shrine*
*Expensive but convenient. High stock.*
- **Super Potato:** 3 floors of retro heaven. Great for photos, pricey for buying.
- **Trader Headquarters:** Better prices than Super Potato. Check the "Junk" bins on the 2nd floor.
- **Surugaya:** Chaos. Digging required. Often has the best hidden gems.
- **Retro Game Camp:** specialized in battery replacement. Good for functional carts.
- **BEEP:** PC-98 and rare vintage computers. Underground.

### 2. Nakano Broadway (Tokyo) - *The Collector's Den*
*Niche, rare, and weird.*
- **Mandarake Galaxy:** The specific video game store. Rare boxed items.
- **Mandarake Deep:** Vintage toys and obscure electronics.

### 3. Den Den Town (Osaka) - *The "Real" Hunt*
*Often cheaper than Tokyo.*
- **Super Potato Osaka:** Less crowded, stock moves slower.
- **Game Tanteidan:** "Detective Agency". A must-visit for serious hunters.
- **Joshin Super Kids Land:** New stuff (Gundam/Plamo), but essential for supplies.

##  The "Hard Off" Strategy (Suburbs)

*If you have time, leave the city center.*
**Hard Off / Book Off / Hobby Off:** Nationwide recycle shops.
- **The "Blue Bin":** The "Junk" section (Janku).
  - *Consoles:* 500 - 2000 ($3 - $13). Usually just dirty, often works.
  - *Carts:* 100 - 500 ($0.65 - $3). No box, loose.
- **Labeling:**
  - "Powers on" (Dentsu shimashita) = Works.
  - "Unchecked" (Mikaikets) = Gamble.
  - "Broken" (Kowareteimasu) = For parts.

##  Region Locking Guide

*Will it work on my US TV/Console?*

| System | Locking Status | Plan |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Game Boy / GBA / DS** |  **Region Free** | Works on any system! Buy freely. |
| **PSP / Vita** |  **Region Free** | Works on any system. |
| **PS3 / PS4 / Switch** |  **Region Free** | Works on any system. |
| **Famicom (NES)** |  **Locked** | Cart shape different. Need adapter or Japanese console. |
| **Super Famicom (SNES)** |  **Locked** | Physical tabs in console block cart. File tabs off or buy adapter. |
| **Nintendo 64** |  **Locked** | Physical tabs. Swap back plastic tray or use GameShark. |
| **GameCube** |  **Locked** | Software lock. Need Freeloader disc or mod. |
| **Sega Genesis (Mega Drive)** |  **Locked** | Cart shape + Software. Varies by game. |
| **Sega Saturn / Dreamcast** |  **Locked** | Software locked. Need boot disc or ode mod. |

**Power Voltage:**
- Japan: 100V.
- US: 110-120V.
- *Result:* Most vintage Japanese consoles work fine in the US without a step-down converter, but they run slightly hot. For long-term preservation, buy a cheap step-down converter ($30).

##  Buying Phrases

- **"Kore wa ugokimasu ka?"** (Does this work?)
- **"Nakami o misete moraemasu ka?"** (Can I see inside? - For boxed games)
- **"Menzei dekimasu ka?"** (Can I do tax-free?)
- **"Janku desu ka?"** (Is this junk/broken?)

##  Packing Strategy

1. **Handhelds:** Carry-on (Lithium batteries must be in cabin!).
2. **Consoles:** Checked bag. Bubble wrap (buy at 100 yen shop) + Clothes.
3. **Cartridges:** Carry-on or Checked. Very durable.
4. **Disc Games:** CD cases crack easily. Wrap in shirts.
