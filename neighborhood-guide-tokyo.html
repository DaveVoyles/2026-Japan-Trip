🌆 Shinjuku (The Neon Jungle)

Vibe: Tokyo at full volume: neon, crowds, food, nightlife, and one of the city’s easiest launchpads. East side is chaotic and fun; west side is calmer and more businesslike. 📺 4K Walk

Highlights:

  • Kabukicho and the Godzilla Head (Shinjuku's famous neon entertainment district — arcades, karaoke bars, and the Hotel Gracery Godzilla head on the roof) for classic neon-Tokyo energy.
  • Shinjuku Gyoen (a large national garden — 144 acres, one of the biggest parks in central Tokyo) for a reset when the station area gets intense.
  • Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building (city hall twin-towers with free public observation decks on the 45th floor) for impressive city views.
  • Isetan Shinjuku depachika (the department store's basement food hall — fresh bento, pastries, sweets, and gift boxes) for excellent pre-packed meals and souvenirs.

Getting There: You’re already based here. From Keio Plaza, most Shinjuku spots are a 10-20 minute walk depending on which side of the station you want.

Pro Tips: Use the west or south side when you want a calmer entry/exit. Kabukicho is fun, but ignore anyone pitching bars or "cheap drinks."

🌃 Best used as your dinner-and-night-walk district, not your first place for a quiet morning.

🚥 Shibuya (The Youth Pulse)

Vibe: Loud, stylish, and youthful — the city’s most recognizable burst of street life. Great for people-watching, big views, and flagship stores. 📺 4K Walk

Highlights:

  • Shibuya Scramble and the Hachiko statue (Hachiko is a bronze Akita dog who waited at this station every day for his owner for years — Tokyo's most iconic meeting point).
  • Shibuya Sky (an open-air rooftop observation deck on top of Scramble Square, 46 floors up) for one of the best views in Tokyo.
  • Miyashita Park (a rooftop park built on top of a shopping complex — benches, food stalls, and a skate park) for a break between stops.
  • Shibuya Parco for Nintendo Tokyo, Pokémon Center, and other pop-culture shops.

Getting There: JR Yamanote Line from Shinjuku to Shibuya takes about 7 minutes. If you start in Harajuku, you can walk down Cat Street into Shibuya.

Pro Tips: Book Shibuya Sky weeks ahead if you want sunset. Coin lockers are plentiful in the station, but the area is most enjoyable if you arrive light.

📷 For the classic crossing photo, use the area around Scramble Square or magnet-facing viewpoints instead of stopping in the middle.

👟 Harajuku (Streetwear & Cat Street)

Vibe: Fashion extremes in one compact zone: hyper-busy Takeshita Street, cool side-street boutiques, and leafy shrine grounds nearby. 📺 4K Walk

Highlights:

  • Takeshita Street (a 400m pedestrian lane packed with kawaii fashion, crepe stands, and snack shops — Japan's youth streetwear hub) for the full spectacle.
  • Cat Street (a quieter indie boutique lane running parallel to Takeshita — much calmer and more curated) for better-paced vintage shopping and streetwear.
  • Omotesando (Tokyo's upscale tree-lined boulevard — often called the Japanese Champs-Élysées) for flagship stores, architecture, and a more polished walk.
  • Coffee stops and hidden boutiques tucked off the main lanes.

Getting There: JR Yamanote Line from Shinjuku to Harajuku is about 4 minutes. Harajuku also connects naturally to Meiji Jingu and a downhill walk into Shibuya.

Pro Tips: Takeshita gets slammed by midday; do it early or skip straight to Cat Street if you want something more relaxed. Harajuku Station has handy lockers near the Omotesando side.

🛍️ Harajuku works best in the morning, then roll south into Shibuya as the day gets busier.

👜 Ginza (The Polished Heart)

Vibe: Tokyo’s refined shopping district — quieter, cleaner-lined, and much less frantic than the west-side nightlife hubs. 📺 4K Walk

Highlights:

  • Weekend Chuo-dori pedestrian paradise when the main street closes to cars.
  • Ginza Six and Uniqlo Ginza for destination-scale shopping.
  • Kabuki-za Theatre area (Japan's most famous kabuki theater — traditional stylized stage drama performed since 1889, with a striking temple-style facade worth seeing even from outside) for a culture stop.
  • Mitsukoshi Ginza depachika (flagship department store basement food hall — prepared foods, wagashi sweets, and premium bento) and Itoya for food gifts and stationery.

Getting There: Take the Marunouchi Line from Shinjuku to Ginza in roughly 20 minutes. It’s also close to Tokyo Station if you’re pairing it with Shinkansen logistics.

Pro Tips: Carry your passport for tax-free shopping over ¥5,500. If you want a calmer Tokyo shopping day, choose Ginza over Shibuya.

🛍️ Great rainy-day district: department stores, food halls, and covered shopping mean you can stay productive without a perfect forecast.

⛩️ Asakusa & Akihabara (Temples & Tech)

Vibe: One of the best Tokyo pairings: old Tokyo temple atmosphere in Asakusa, then neon hobby-and-arcade energy in Akihabara. 📺 4K Walk

Highlights:

  • Senso-ji and Nakamise — Senso-ji is one of Tokyo's oldest Buddhist temples (founded 645 AD); Nakamise is the 250m shop-lined approach lane leading to the main gate — for the classic first-timer temple walk.
  • Asakusa Culture Tourist Information Center for a free observation deck.
  • Super Potato, Taito Station, and BEEP (multi-floor retro game shops — Super Potato is the flagship for vintage games and consoles going back to the Famicom era) in Akihabara for retro-game browsing.
  • Kappabashi Kitchen Town (a wholesale street for professional knives, ceramics, and those hyper-realistic plastic food display models used in restaurant windows) for cookware and unique souvenirs.

Getting There: Easiest plan from Shinjuku is JR to Akihabara in about 20 minutes, then hop onward to Asakusa or reverse it depending on your day. This area is best done as a dedicated east-side outing.

Pro Tips: Do Senso-ji by 6:30 AM if you want quiet temple time, and remember there’s no photography inside the main hall. Akihabara shops usually don’t get going until around 11:00 AM; Kappabashi tends to close early around 5:00 PM.

🎮 Ideal combo: early Asakusa, late-morning Kappabashi, then Akihabara once the gaming shops are actually open.

🖼️ Roppongi (The Art Triangle)

Vibe: More international, more polished, and strongly museum-centered by day — then more nightlife-heavy after dark. 📺 4K Walk

Highlights:

  • Mori Art Museum (contemporary art museum on the 53rd floor of Roppongi Hills Mori Tower) for world-class shows and panoramic views.
  • National Art Center for big rotating exhibitions and standout architecture.
  • Suntory Museum of Art in Tokyo Midtown for traditional craft and design.
  • Tokyo City View for one of the better skyline lookouts.

Getting There: From the hotel, walk to Tochomae or Shinjuku-area Oedo Line access and ride direct to Roppongi in about 10-12 minutes.

Pro Tips: Check museum closure days before you go, and do not follow any street tout into a bar here or in Kabukicho. Roppongi is strongest as an afternoon-into-evening plan.

⚠️ Best version of Roppongi: museum first, skyline second, drinks only in places you picked yourself.

🚉 Shinjuku Station (Train Hub)

Vibe: Functional chaos — the busiest rail station in the world, but incredibly useful once you pick the right exit and line. Google Maps

Highlights:

  • Direct access to JR, subway, private rail, and long-distance day-trip routes.
  • Fast connections to Shibuya, Harajuku, Akihabara, and Tokyo Station.
  • Department stores, food halls, and shopping wrapped around the station complex.
  • Useful split personalities: east for energy, west for hotels/skyscrapers, south for shopping.

Getting There: From Keio Plaza, this is your home station area — roughly a 10-15 minute walk depending on which entrance or underground passage you use.

Pro Tips: Decide your exit before you leave the platform. Avoid rush hour with luggage if possible, and use coin lockers only if you truly need them — navigating is easier when you travel light.

🧭 If you get disoriented, reset by following signs for a major exit rather than trying to improvise through the underground maze.

🍢 Omoide Yokocho ("Memory Lane")

Vibe: Narrow, smoky, nostalgic, and one of the easiest ways to get a tiny-skillet, postwar-alley Tokyo feeling without leaving central Shinjuku. 📺 4K Walk

Highlights:

  • Rows of miniature yakitori grills and izakaya counters (izakaya = casual Japanese bar/pub with small plates — the kind of place you share skewers and cold beer).
  • Great atmosphere for a short snack-and-beer stop after dark.
  • Retro lantern-lit photo moments tucked right beside the station.
  • Easy add-on to a Kabukicho or west-side Shinjuku night.

Getting There: Walk from the hotel toward Shinjuku Station’s west/northwest side; it’s an easy 15-minute Shinjuku evening add-on.

Pro Tips: Go with a small group, expect tight seating, and keep cash or a backup card handy. It’s more about atmosphere than a long, leisurely meal.

🍺 Best move: one or two skewers here, then continue elsewhere if you want a fuller dinner.

🌲 Meiji Jingu

Vibe: A surprisingly quiet, forested shrine retreat that feels far removed from nearby Harajuku crowds. 📺 4K Walk

Highlights:

  • The giant torii approach (torii = traditional Shinto shrine archways, these are among the largest wooden ones in Japan) and long wooded paths through 70 hectares of forest.
  • Main shrine buildings and broad gravel courtyards (the shrine is dedicated to Emperor Meiji and Empress Shoken, built in 1920 — the most important Shinto shrine in Tokyo).
  • A peaceful cultural contrast to the shopping streets next door.
  • Easy pairing with Harajuku or Omotesando on the same outing.

Getting There: JR Yamanote from Shinjuku to Harajuku takes about 4 minutes, then it’s a short walk to the shrine entrances. A taxi is also easy if you want a simpler start.

Pro Tips: Bow once before entering the torii, walk on the sides of the path rather than the center, and go early for the calmest visit.

🙏 Strong morning opener before Harajuku shops wake up.

🎟️ Klook Bookable Experiences

Overview: These activities can be pre-booked on Klook for skip-the-line access or guaranteed entry. Mix of temples, unique cultural experiences, and observation decks to fill gaps and free time.

🔴 Book early: Sumo Morning Practice has limited daily capacity. Tokyo Skytree and Tower save ¥200–400 vs. walk-up.

Temples & Shrines:

  • Zojoji Temple + Tokyo Tower — Historic Buddhist temple (1393) in Shiba Park with Tokyo Tower looming directly behind the gate. The Sangedatsumon Gate (1622) survived WWII. Haunting row of Jizo statues in red bibs. The most iconic photo composition in Tokyo. Temple is free; combine with Tokyo Tower (Klook #4911, ~¥3,300 both decks, saves ¥200). Open until 23:00. Map
  • Gotokuji Temple (Lucky Cat Temple) — Zen Buddhist temple in Setagaya, birthplace of the maneki-neko (lucky cat). Hundreds of white ceramic cats stacked on shelves in a surreal, photogenic display. 15 min from Shinjuku via Odakyu Line. Free admission. Most tourists miss it entirely. Map
  • Nezu Shrine (Tokyo's Secret Fushimi Inari) — 1,900-year-old Shinto shrine with a hillside torii gate tunnel nearly identical to Kyoto's Fushimi Inari but with 1/10th the crowds. Main hall (1706) is a National Treasure. 5 min from Nezu Station (Chiyoda Line). Free. Combine with a Yanaka old-town walk (15 min away). Map

Unique Experiences:

  • Sumo Morning Practice Tour ⭐ — English-guided visit to a sumo stable in Ryogoku. Watch wrestlers train for 2–3 hours in an intimate setting. ~20 min from Shinagawa. Klook #93976, ~¥10,000/person. Best for Jun 2 (Day 9). Map

Observation Decks:

  • Tokyo Skytree (Both Decks) — Japan's tallest structure at 634m. Two observation decks at 350m and 450m with a spiral skywalk. Near Ryogoku — combine with sumo morning. Klook #41352, ~¥3,100 both decks (saves ¥300–400 vs. walk-up). Best for Jun 2. Map
  • Tokyo Tower (Both Decks) — Classic 333m landmark, open until 23:00. The 250m top deck is Tokyo's 3rd highest viewpoint with Mt. Fuji views on clear days. Klook #4911, ~¥3,300 (saves ¥200). Best paired with Zojoji Temple. Map

📚 Places & References (English Guide)

Shibuya & Harajuku

Meiji Jingu (Meiji Shrine)
明治神宮
One of Japan's most important Shinto shrines, dedicated to the Emperor Meiji. It's surrounded by a peaceful, forested grove and accessed by walking under traditional torii gates. The shrine features broad gravel courtyards and is a popular place to draw fortune-telling papers (omikuji). Locals bow at the entrance torii gate as a sign of respect.
Days 2-3: Morning visit recommended (before 10 AM for calm)
Takeshita Street
竹下通り
Harajuku's most famous pedestrian shopping street, packed with fashion boutiques, crepe stands, and kawaii (cute) culture. It's narrow, crowded, and quintessential youth-Tokyo. Morning visits are quieter than afternoons. The street stretches about 400 meters from Harajuku Station and connects to nearby cafes and shops.
Day 2: Morning preferred (before noon)
Cat Street (Omotesando Kodo)
キャットストリート
A quieter, more upscale shopping street in Harajuku that runs parallel to Takeshita. Known for vintage boutiques, independent designers, and artisanal cafes. It has a relaxed, refined vibe compared to Takeshita Street's frenzy. A great spot to discover unique fashion and escape crowds.
Day 2: Mid-morning to midday transition
Shibuya Scramble Crossing
渋谷スクランブル交差点
The world's busiest pedestrian crossing, filmed countless times in movies and shows. Thousands of people cross simultaneously at this intersection. It's iconic Tokyo and a must-photograph moment. Best views from the Starbucks on the corner or the pedestrian bridge nearby. Photos are most dramatic at dusk.
Day 2: Late afternoon (around 3-5 PM)
Shibuya Sky
渋谷スカイ
A 46-story observation deck located atop Scramble Square offering 360-degree views of Tokyo from one of the tallest buildings in the area. The viewing floor is open-air and gives a stunning perspective of the city's density and scale. Pre-booking is essential, especially for sunset or evening slots.
Day 2: 2:00 PM (Booking QSJ919058 confirmed)
Hachiko Statue
忠犬ハチ公像
A statue of Hachiko, a famous Akita dog known for his loyalty to his owner. Located outside Shibuya Station, it's one of Tokyo's most recognizable meeting points and photo spots. The statue has become a symbol of loyalty and is always crowded with tourists and locals.
Day 2: Near Shibuya Scramble visit
Shibuya Parco
渋谷パルコ
A major fashion and entertainment shopping center in Shibuya housing flagship stores like Nintendo Tokyo and Pokémon Center. Multiple floors feature clothing, electronics, and pop-culture merchandise. It's one of Japan's premier pop-culture retail destinations.
Day 2: Evening shopping (after Shibuya Sky)
Nintendo Tokyo
ニンテンドウ トウキョウ
An official Nintendo experience store inside Shibuya Parco featuring the latest games, exclusive merchandise, and interactive displays. It's a pilgrimage destination for gaming fans and a great place to find hard-to-find Nintendo products not available internationally.
Day 2: Shibuya Parco visit (if time allows)
Pokémon Center
ポケモンセンター
An official Pokémon merchandise store inside Shibuya Parco with exclusive Japanese Pokémon items, collectibles, and region-specific products. Shelves are stocked with cards, toys, apparel, and gifts not found in Western stores. Known for limited-edition collaborations with other brands.
Day 2: Shibuya Parco visit (pop-culture stop)
Marion Crepes
マリオンクレープ
A famous crepe stand on Takeshita Street known for sweet and savory crepes made to order. Fluffy crepes filled with whipped cream, Nutella, strawberries, and other toppings are a Harajuku tradition. Popular with tourists and locals for a quick, inexpensive sweet treat.
Day 2: Harajuku midday (crepe break)
@Cosme Tokyo
@コスメ トウキョウ
A large cosmetics and beauty products flagship store in Harajuku showcasing Japanese and international skincare, makeup, and wellness products. It's a paradise for beauty enthusiasts and one of the best places to find cult Japanese beauty items (sheet masks, serums, face rollers) at reasonable prices.
Day 2: Harajuku shopping (Debbie's preference)

Shinjuku

Kabukicho
歌舞伎町
Shinjuku's famous entertainment and red-light district known for its neon signs, pachinko parlors, karaoke, bars, and restaurants. It's one of Tokyo's most lively night-time areas with tremendous energy and a classic "neon Tokyo" aesthetic. Daytime is quieter; nighttime is buzzing with activity.
Evenings: Part of Shinjuku nightlife exploration
Godzilla Head
ゴジラヘッド
A famous Godzilla sculpture mounted on the roof of a building in Kabukicho, right next to Shinjuku Station. It's become an iconic photo spot and symbol of Shinjuku's pop-culture energy. The head occasionally "roars" with sound and light effects, adding to its theatrical appeal.
Evenings or rainy day photo opportunity
Shinjuku Gyoen
新宿御苑
A large, peaceful urban park in Shinjuku featuring landscaped gardens, trees, and open greenspace. It offers a quiet retreat from the chaotic Shinjuku streets and is especially beautiful during cherry blossom season. The park is free to enter and includes a tea house where you can rest.
Anytime for a peaceful break from crowds
Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building
東京都庁
A twin-tower government building in Shinjuku with free observation decks on the 45th floor offering panoramic views of Tokyo and beyond. On clear days, you can see Mount Fuji from the north observation deck. It's free and accessible to the public, making it an excellent budget-friendly alternative to paid observation decks.
Anytime, especially for free city views
Isetan Shinjuku
伊勢丹新宿
A flagship department store in Shinjuku known for its food halls (depachika) featuring premium bento, Japanese sweets, and gift items. The depachika is particularly famous for high-quality prepared foods and beautiful gift packaging. Excellent place to find souvenirs and snacks.
Anytime for gift shopping or food hall exploration
Omoide Yokocho
思い出横丁
A narrow alleyway in Shinjuku lined with tiny yakitori (grilled chicken) and izakaya (casual bar) restaurants. The nostalgic, postwar atmosphere with paper lanterns and intimate seating makes it feel like stepping back in time. It's perfect for a quick skewer and beer experience in an authentically retro setting.
Evenings for atmospheric yakitori snack

Ginza & Nihonbashi

Ginza Six
ギンザシックス
A luxury shopping complex in Ginza with designer boutiques, restaurants, and a rooftop garden. It combines contemporary architecture with high-end retail and is a symbol of Ginza's refined shopping culture. The building also houses art exhibitions and seasonal events.
Day 3: Afternoon Ginza shopping
Uniqlo Ginza
ユニクロ銀座
A flagship Uniqlo store in Ginza showcasing the brand's full range of minimalist, quality basics (shirts, jeans, thermal wear). The Ginza location is particularly large and well-stocked. Great for basic clothing at reasonable prices or trying the latest seasonal collections.
Day 3: Afternoon Ginza shopping (Debbie's preference)
Itoya
伊藤屋
A legendary nine-story stationery and specialty goods store in Ginza. It stocks premium pens, paper, notebooks, washi tape, and creative supplies from around the world. A paradise for stationary lovers and gift-seekers looking for beautiful, unique Japanese items.
Day 3: Afternoon Ginza shopping (stationery/gifts)
Chuo-dori
中央通り
Ginza's main shopping boulevard that closes to car traffic on weekends, becoming a pedestrian paradise. Lined with luxury boutiques, galleries, and cafes, it's one of Tokyo's most refined shopping experiences. The street is wide, clean, and elegant—a stark contrast to the chaos of Shinjuku.
Weekends or weekday afternoons for shopping
Kabuki-za Theatre
歌舞伎座
Historic kabuki theater in Ginza where traditional Japanese kabuki performances are held. Even if you don't see a full performance, the ornate architecture is worth viewing. Single-act tickets allow you to watch just one part of a performance without committing to a full evening.
Optional cultural stop in Ginza area
Mitsukoshi Ginza
三越銀座
A historic high-end department store in Ginza known for its food halls (depachika) with premium prepared foods, desserts, and gift items. The store embodies Japanese luxury and craftsmanship. Perfect for finding quality gifts, seasonal sweets, and prepared meals.
Anytime for gift shopping and food hall browsing

Asakusa & Akihabara

Senso-ji Temple
浅草寺
Tokyo's oldest temple, founded in 645, dedicated to Avalokiteshvara (Kannon), the bodhisattva of mercy. It features a massive red lantern (chōchin), traditional architecture, and gates. Visitors can draw fortune-telling papers (omikuji) and observe locals making offerings. The main hall is off-limits to photography, but the grounds are spectacular.
Day 4: Early morning (before 6:30 AM for quiet)
Nakamise Shopping Street
仲見世通り
A covered shopping arcade leading to Senso-ji Temple selling traditional souvenirs, snacks, toys, and temple-related goods. The street is busy but offers a slice of old Tokyo commerce and culture. It's a great place to pick up small gifts and try traditional Japanese snacks.
Day 4: Morning with Senso-ji Temple visit
Asakusa Culture Tourist Information Center
浅草文化観光センター
A modern information center with a free observation deck on the 8th floor offering views of Senso-ji Temple and the surrounding area. Helpful staff can provide local maps and recommendations. The observation deck is free and offers a different perspective of Asakusa's traditional streetscape.
Day 4: Near Senso-ji Temple visit
Yanaka Ginza
谷中銀座
A charming, narrow shopping street in Yanaka that preserves prewar Tokyo's residential character. Lined with small family-run shops, cafes, and restaurants, it feels like a neighborhood frozen in time. The pace is relaxed and the atmosphere is intimate—perfect for experiencing old Tokyo away from tourist crowds.
Day 4: Morning walk before Senso-ji (pre-war exploration)
Kappabashi Kitchen Town
合羽橋道具街
A specialized wholesale and retail street famous for restaurant equipment, beautiful ceramics, knives, and plastic food display models. The fake food samples (food models) are intricately crafted and collectible. It's a unique shopping experience showcasing traditional Japanese craftsmanship and culinary culture.
Day 4: Between Asakusa and Akihabara (optional short stop)
Super Potato
スーパーポテト
A famous retro video game shop in Akihabara specializing in vintage consoles, games, and collectibles. Multi-floor store packed with treasures for retro gaming enthusiasts (Famicom, SNES, Sega, etc.). A paradise for Dave's retro gaming interests with rare finds and fair prices.
Day 4: Afternoon Akihabara (Dave's main stop)
Taito Station
タイトーステーション
A large arcade chain featuring UFO catcher games (claw machines), rhythm games (MaiMai, Taiko), retro video games, and gachapon (capsule toy) machines. Multiple floors of gaming fun with clean facilities and a steady supply of new games. Great for spending coins and trying arcade games you won't find elsewhere.
Day 4: Afternoon Akihabara (gachapon & arcade games)
Mandarake Complex
まんだらけコンプレックス
A multi-floor otaku (anime/manga enthusiast) superstore in Akihabara selling anime, manga, figures, collectibles, and memorabilia. Each floor is dedicated to a different category (Doujinshi, figures, vintage anime, etc.). A comprehensive destination for collecting anime and pop-culture items.
Day 4: Afternoon Akihabara (otaku collectibles)
Kanda Myojin Shrine
神田明神
One of Tokyo's oldest and most important shrines (established 730 AD), recently popular with anime fans due to its appearance in the anime "A Certain Magical Index." It blends historical significance with pop-culture appeal. Visitors can purchase ema (wooden prayer plaques) and amulets related to both traditional and anime themes.
Day 4: Optional Akihabara add-on (anime shrine)

Roppongi & Nihonbashi

Mori Art Museum
森美術館
A contemporary art museum located on the 52-53rd floors of Roppongi Hills with rotating exhibitions of contemporary and international art. Admission includes access to Tokyo City View observation deck with 360-degree panoramic views. The museum is highly regarded for its curated shows and the views alone are worth the visit.
Day 3: Evening visit (5 PM), optional based on energy
National Art Center
国立新美術館
Japan's largest art museum by area, located in Roppongi with rotating exhibitions of contemporary, international, and traditional art. The architecture (by Kisho Kurokawa) is striking with a distinctive wave-shaped glass exterior. Free entry to the building and some exhibitions, paid admission for special exhibitions.
Optional Roppongi museum visit
Suntory Museum of Art
サントリー美術館
A museum in Tokyo Midtown focused on Japanese traditional crafts, design, and contemporary art. Known for its refined exhibitions of ceramics, lacquerware, and textiles. The museum emphasizes the intersection of fine art and everyday beauty in Japanese culture.
Optional Roppongi museum visit
Tokyo Midtown
東京ミッドタウン
A large mixed-use complex in Roppongi with office towers, shops, restaurants, and the Suntory Museum. The complex has a modern, upscale feel with high-end retail and dining. Beautiful in the evening with illuminated greenspace and outdoor seating areas.
Roppongi evening destination for museums and dining
Tokyo City View
東京シティビュー
An observation deck on the 52nd floor of Roppongi Hills offering panoramic 360-degree views of Tokyo. Admission is included with Mori Art Museum entry or available separately. Known for having one of the best unobstructed skyline views in Tokyo, especially beautiful at night.
Day 3: Optional with Mori Art Museum visit

Markets & Restaurants

Toyosu Market
豊洲市場
Tokyo's main wholesale fish and produce market (moved from Tsukiji). The market has a public area where visitors can eat fresh sushi and seafood at specialized restaurants. Early morning (7-10 AM) offers the most energy and freshest selections. A fascinating look at Tokyo's food supply chain and a great place to eat exceptional sushi at market prices.
Day 3: Morning/brunch (10:30 AM)
Daiwa Sushi
大和寿司
A famous sushi counter in Toyosu Market serving omakase (chef's choice) with the freshest fish available that morning. Diners sit directly at the counter and the chef prepares pieces to order. Known for exceptional quality and a lively market atmosphere. Expect lines, but the experience is authentic and memorable.
Day 3: Morning/brunch at Toyosu Market
Sushi Dai
寿司大
Another highly-regarded sushi counter in Toyosu Market offering fresh omakase with excellent value. Similar to Daiwa Sushi, this is a no-frills, quality-first experience with a dedicated sushi chef. Lines are long (especially on weekends), but turnover is quick and the fresh fish makes the wait worthwhile.
Day 3: Morning/brunch at Toyosu Market (alternative)
Tempura Daikokuya
天麩羅大黒屋
A traditional tempura restaurant in Asakusa with a casual counter and cozy atmosphere. Known for light, crispy tempura and attentive service. A great spot for a quick lunch after visiting Senso-ji Temple. No English menu, but photos and gestures work well.
Day 4: Lunch around Asakusa (12 PM)
Ichiran Ramen
一蘭ラーメン
A famous tonkotsu (pork bone) ramen chain with a location in Shibuya. Known for smooth, creamy broth and thin noodles. Each seat is a private booth (you choose your broth intensity and toppings before eating). Fast, efficient, and delicious—a quintessential Tokyo quick-meal experience.
Day 2: Evening dinner (8 PM) in Shibuya
