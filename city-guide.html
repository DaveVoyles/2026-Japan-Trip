Neighborhoods

  • Free observation deck at Metro Govt Building

Shibuya 🔀
Youth Pulse — iconic crossing & culture
  • Scramble Crossing (world's busiest)
  • Hachiko statue meeting point
  • Shibuya Parco: Nintendo Tokyo, Pokemon Center

  • Hands/Loft for practical shopping after Shibuya Sky

Harajuku & Cat Street 🎀
Youth fashion — kawaii to high streetwear

  • Cat Street — indie boutiques, cafes, vintage (much calmer than Takeshita)

Yanaka 🏮
Pre-war Tokyo — quiet & nostalgic Shitamachi

  • Tennoji Temple & bronze Buddha
  • Yuyake Dandan "Sunset Stairs"
  • Nippori Fabric Town nearby

Roppongi 🎨

  • Mori Art Museum (53rd floor, open until 10 PM)

  • Suntory Museum of Art — traditional Japanese craft

Free
Meiji Jingu Shrine
A serene forested Shinto shrine in the heart of Tokyo — a calm contrast to Harajuku just steps away. Day 2 morning anchor.
Dedicated to Emperor Meiji and Empress Shoken. The forested path through the torii gate feels miles from the city. Arrive before 9 AM to beat tour groups. Iris garden blooms in June. Look for sake barrels gifted from French wineries lined along the path.
✅ Free; genuinely peaceful in a dense city; easy from Harajuku Station ⚠️ Gets crowded mid-morning; no indoor exhibits
Walk-in Opens at sunrise English: High
Free
Senso-ji Temple
Tokyo's oldest and most famous temple in the Asakusa district — the iconic red lantern gate. Day 4 anchor.
The Kaminarimon (Thunder Gate) with its giant red lantern is one of the most photographed spots in Japan. Nakamise-dori shopping street leads up to the main hall. Draw an Omikuji (fortune slip) — if bad luck, tie it to the rack to leave it behind. The 5-story pagoda is nearby and worth a photo.
✅ Free; iconic photo spots; great souvenir shopping on Nakamise ⚠️ Can be very crowded by 10 AM; some stalls are touristy
Walk-in Open 24hrs (grounds) English: Medium

Shinkansen (Bullet Train)
Japan's high-speed rail network — for this trip, the key route is Tokyo → Kyoto → Osaka on the Tokaido Shinkansen.
Nozomi trains are the fastest at about 2h15m from Tokyo to Kyoto, but they do not accept the JR Pass. Hikari is slightly slower at about 2h40m and does accept the JR Pass. Tickets can be reserved at any JR ticket office (みどりの窓口) or at Shinkansen vending machines. Reserve a window seat (madogawa) and specify direction for Mt. Fuji views: A/B seats heading west, D/E seats heading east. At Tokyo Station, enter through the Tokaido Shinkansen gates and buy ekiben at Gransta before going through, since food options inside are limited. At Shin-Osaka, follow the Shinkansen signs from the Osaka Metro exit — it's a dedicated Shinkansen hub, not a regular metro stop. Large bags officially should not live in the overhead racks unless they fit, so oversized luggage reservations or Yamato takkyubin forwarding (about ¥1,500–2,000 per bag) are the cleaner play. Mt. Fuji appears about 40 minutes after leaving Tokyo on the left side heading west, and on the right side heading east back toward Tokyo.
✅ Fastest intercity option, easy reservations, and great Mt. Fuji window-seat payoff ⚠️ Nozomi/JR Pass rules and oversized luggage policies can trip you up
Seat Reservation Recommended Tokaido Shinkansen English: High

Toyosu Market
The modern replacement for Tsukiji — Japan's largest wholesale fish market with inner-market tuna auctions. Day 3 morning.
The tuna auction viewing is lottery-based (apply months ahead) but the outer market restaurants are open to all. Arrive hungry — sushi breakfasts here are among the best in Tokyo. Daiwa Sushi and Sushi Dai have long lines but are worth it. The market itself is viewable from glass walkways.
✅ Freshest sushi in Tokyo; interesting working market atmosphere ⚠️ Lines at top counters can be 1–2 hours; closes early (~2 PM)

Omoide Yokocho (Memory Lane / Piss Alley)
A narrow smoky alleyway in Shinjuku packed with tiny yakitori bars — one of Tokyo's most atmospheric first-night dinner spots.
Dating back to post-WWII, Omoide Yokocho is a maze of tiny yakitori bars, most with only 6–8 seats. Smoke from charcoal grills fills the lane; cold beer and grilled chicken skewers are the move. Located immediately west of Shinjuku Station north exit, it comes alive from around 5–6 PM. Most places are cash only, and there are no reservations — just walk up and grab an open seat. It's perfect for a low-stakes first-night dinner after a long travel day since you can simply point at what other people are eating. Go before 7 PM to avoid the longest lines; the alley is tiny, so a 10-minute wait is normal.
✅ Atmospheric, easy first-night dinner, and minimal menu navigation needed ⚠️ Tiny seating, smoky lanes, and brief waits are normal

Tips & Essentials

🚆 Getting Around

  • Get a Suica card at airport or add to Apple Wallet before arrival
  • JR Yamanote Line (green loop) is your lifeline
  • Stand on the LEFT on escalators
  • Avoid rush hour 7:30–9:30 AM with luggage

⛩️ Temple Tips

  • Senso-ji: arrive 6:30 AM to beat the crowds
  • Meiji Shrine: bow before the giant Torii gates
  • Shibuya Sky: book 4+ weeks ahead; sunset sells out instantly
  • Akihabara shops don't open until 11 AM

🛍️ Shopping

  • Carry passport for tax-free on purchases over ¥5,500
  • Basement food halls (Depachika) at Isetan Shinjuku or Mitsukoshi Ginza are unmissable
  • Coin lockers at major stations for day-bag drops

🍱 Food Culture

  • No eating while walking — find a spot or eat at the stall
  • Public trash cans are rare; carry a small bag
  • 7-Eleven or Japan Post Bank ATMs for reliable cash

Nara Park
Wide open park famous for bowing deer — Day 6's top-priority experience.
Buy deer crackers only when you're ready to feed immediately. The deer recognize the wrapper sound, so keep moving and show open empty hands when you're out of food. Watch for droppings and give yourself time to enjoy the park instead of rushing it.
✅ Memorable, easygoing, and very walkable ⚠️ Deer can swarm once crackers appear

Cross Hotel Kyoto, Kyoto Station, Kyoto-Kawaramachi, Fushimi access, and the Nara transfer.
Cross Hotel Kyoto is the Kyoto base for Nishiki, Gion, Pontocho, and the Osaka transfer. Kyoto Station is the Shinkansen arrival point and Nara launch point when using Kintetsu. Kyoto-Kawaramachi is the easy hotel-side station for the Hankyu Kyoto Line to Osaka-Umeda. For Day 6, Kintetsu Nara Station is closer to Nara Park than JR Nara; for Day 7, Fushimi Inari Station is the simple shrine access point.
✅ Clarifies which station matters for each Kyoto move ⚠️ Kyoto Station and Kyoto-Kawaramachi are different transfer strategies

Tips & Essentials

⏰ Timing

  • Temples busiest 10 AM – 3 PM; arrive at opening (8:30 AM) or 1 hr before closing
  • Bamboo Grove: before 7:30 AM only
  • Fushimi Inari: sunrise or after 4 PM for fewer crowds

💴 Cash & Access

  • Many small temples and shops are cash-only — carry ¥10,000 daily
  • 7-Eleven ATM near Gion Shijo Station accepts foreign cards
  • Kyoto City Bus day pass (¥700) if taking 3+ rides

📸 Photo Etiquette

  • Never photograph inside temple buildings
  • Never photograph Geisha/Maiko without permission
  • Some Gion lanes have photography bans — respect signage

🍃 May Highlight

  • Shinryoku (Fresh Green): maple leaves glow bright green — best at Arashiyama and Tofukuji
  • Kamogawa Yuka: riverside open-air dinner decks along Pontocho from May onwards — book ahead

Kuromon Market
300+ vendors of fresh seafood, meats, and produce — "Osaka's Kitchen." Day 8 brunch.
Osaka's version of Nishiki Market but more raw and lively. Great for fresh uni (sea urchin), wagyu skewers, and giant scallops grilled on the spot. Arrive hungry — most vendors give generous tastings.

A convenient first Osaka snack after check-in. Eat takoyaki while it is hot, but carefully — the center stays molten. If the line is bad, use it as a landmark and pick another busy takoyaki stand nearby.

Rikuro Ojisan Cheesecake
Famous jiggly cheesecake in Namba — they ring a bell when fresh batches emerge.
More soufflé than cheesecake — light, eggy, not too sweet. Watch the staff brand the logo onto the jiggly cakes with a hot iron. Locals recommend chilling it in a fridge to change the texture to a denser cheesecake.

Hozenji Yokocho
A narrow moss-covered alley behind Dotonbori — old Osaka at its most atmospheric. Day 7 evening.
Named for the Hozen-ji temple whose stone deity is covered in moss from decades of water offerings. Lined with tiny traditional izakayas dating back to pre-war Osaka. Perfect for a quiet drink after Dotonbori's neon. Almost no English menus — point and trust.
✅ Authentic old-Osaka feel; great contrast to Dotonbori's chaos ⚠️ Very small restaurants; English menus rare

Shinsekai & Abeno Harukas
Optional south-Osaka branch if Day 8 trades away split-shopping time.
Shinsekai is the retro tower-and-kushikatsu district around Tsutenkaku. Abeno Harukas is the polished 300m observatory nearby in Tennoji. Treat this as an either/or branch with Den Den Town and Shinsaibashi, not an add-on to an already full afternoon.

Osaka Hotel, Namba & Shin-Osaka Logistics
Cross Hotel Osaka, Namba, Osaka-Umeda, Midosuji Line, and Shin-Osaka transfer notes.
Cross Hotel Osaka Dotonbori is the Osaka base, a short walk from Dotonbori/Namba. Kyoto-Kawaramachi to Osaka-Umeda via Hankyu is the easy Day 7 arrival route, then Midosuji Line connects Umeda and Namba. For Day 9, use Namba to Shin-Osaka on the Midosuji Line and protect the fixed Nozomi departure buffer.
✅ Keeps Osaka hotel and station moves in one compact reference ⚠️ Shin-Osaka is not Namba; leave enough platform and gate time

Tips & Essentials

🚶 Escalator Rule

Stand on the RIGHT in Osaka — opposite of Tokyo! Walk on the left.

🍢 Food Rules

  • Kushikatsu: No double dipping into the communal sauce — ever
  • Osaka is "Japan's Kitchen" — street food is always the move
  • Takoyaki: eat them right away, before they cool

🚆 Getting to Tokyo

  • Midosuji Subway to Shin-Osaka, then Shinkansen
  • Book Shinkansen via Smart EX — done!
  • Green Car seat: right side for Fuji views on return

⚠️ Avoid

  • Touts in Dotonbori offering "cheap drinks" — ignore them
  • Missing the last train home — taxis are expensive after midnight
