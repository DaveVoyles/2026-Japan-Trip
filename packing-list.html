💡 Japan-specific notes: Leave room for souvenirs — Japan vacuum bags work well. Many stores don't bag purchases by default anymore. Bring cash for small shrines and markets. Keep passport accessible for tax-free shopping.

🛂 Documents & Money

  • Passport Valid for 6+ months past departure; needed for tax-free shopping
  • Credit / debit cards Visa/Mastercard work widely; notify your bank before you go
  • Cash in JPY ¥50,000–¥70,000 is a reasonable starting amount; ATMs at 7-Eleven and Japan Post accept foreign cards
  • Travel insurance info / card
  • Klook bookings printed or downloaded offline
  • Visit Japan Web QR code saved/printed Pre-register customs/immigration declaration
  • Hotel confirmation numbers saved offline
  • Flight ticket / boarding pass
  • SmartEX / Shinkansen reservations

📱 Tech & Connectivity

  • Ubigi eSIM activated and tested Activate before departure; needs WiFi at first connection
  • Phone charger(s)
  • Portable battery pack (power bank) Long temple/park days drain battery fast; 10,000mAh minimum
  • Universal adapter / Type A plug Japan uses Type A (same as US) — no adapter needed if traveling from US, but check all gear
  • Earbuds or headphones
  • Camera or extra phone lens
  • Extra USB cables
  • Google Translate downloaded for offline Japanese Download Japanese language pack before flying
  • Google Maps offline areas downloaded Download Tokyo, Kyoto, and Osaka before flying

👗 Clothing

🌡️ Late May is warm (22–28°C / 72–82°F) and occasionally rainy. Light layers are key. Temples/shrines may require covered shoulders or removing shoes.
  • T-shirts (light, moisture-wicking) 5–7 depending on laundry access; coin laundry is everywhere in Japan
  • Pants/shorts (2–3 pairs)
  • One smart/semi-formal outfit For nicer restaurants, kaiseki, or Pontocho
  • Light rain jacket / compact umbrella Konbini (convenience stores) sell cheap umbrellas if you forget
  • Comfortable walking shoes You will walk 15,000–25,000 steps a day; break them in beforehand
  • Slip-on shoes or sandals Many temples require removing shoes; slip-ons save time
  • Socks (plenty) Removed at many temple interiors; clean socks required
  • Underwear
  • Sleepwear / loungewear
  • Small daypack or fanny pack Leave luggage at hotel coin lockers; carry only what you need daily

💊 Health & Toiletries

🏥 Japan's pharmacies (Matsumoto Kiyoshi, etc.) sell most Western equivalents. Bring prescription meds from home — foreign prescriptions are not filled in Japan.
  • Prescription medications (full supply + a few extra days)
  • Ibuprofen / pain relief
  • Antidiarrheal / stomach medication
  • Motion sickness tablets For winding bus roads in Arashiyama, etc.
  • Sunscreen SPF 50+ Sold in Japan but buy before leaving; easier and cheaper
  • Insect repellent
  • Toothbrush + toothpaste
  • Deodorant Western strength deodorant is hard to find in Japan; bring your own
  • Shampoo / conditioner (or plan to buy at konbini)
  • Razor
  • Hand sanitizer + travel wipes
  • Small pack of pocket tissues Many Japanese public restrooms don't have paper towels
  • Lip balm

🎒 Day-Trip Essentials

  • Reusable tote bag Japan charges for plastic bags; a compact tote saves ¥5 per shop
  • Reusable water bottle Water fountains are common at shrines and parks; vending machines are everywhere for ¥150
  • Small umbrella Compressible travel umbrella — late May can be rainy
  • Neck pouch / passport holder For passport (needed for tax-free shopping) and IC card
  • Suica / IC card set up on phone (or physical card)
  • Coin purse Japan uses coins heavily — ¥100 and ¥500 coins add up fast at vending machines and shrines
  • Snacks for travel days

📦 Luggage & Organization

  • Main suitcase / checked bag
  • Carry-on bag
  • Luggage lock (TSA-approved)
  • Luggage tag with contact info
  • Compression/vacuum bags for souvenirs Japan purchases will add volume; vacuum bags on the return are very useful
  • Packing cubes or zip-lock bags for organization
  • Separate bag for dirty laundry
