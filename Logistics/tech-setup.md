##  1. Suica / PASMO (Transit Card)

**Why:** Pays for trains, subways, vending machines, konbini snacks, some taxis, and coin lockers.
**Device:** iPhone (Apple Wallet) or Android (Google Pay - limited).
**Initial Load Recommendation:** ¥5,000 ($33) - Covers 3-4 days of local transport.

### Setup Steps (Do this 2 weeks before trip):
1. Open **Apple Wallet**.
2. Tap **+** (Add Card).
3. Select **Transit Card**.
4. Search for **"Suica"** or **"PASMO"** (Either works fine, functionally identical).
5. Add money (Load ¥5,000 to start). *Note: Visa sometimes fails. Mastercard/Amex work best.*
6. **Express Transit Mode:** Go to Settings -> Wallet & Apple Pay -> Express Transit Card -> Select Suica.
   - *Result:* You don't need FaceID to tap through gates. Just tap your locked phone.
   
### 💡 Pro Tips:
- **Balance Check:** Open Apple Wallet, tap Suica card to see balance
- **Reloading:** Tap card → "..." → Add Money (can reload at any time)
- **Where It Works:** All trains/subways, buses, konbini (7-Eleven, Lawson, FamilyMart), vending machines, coin lockers
- **Shinkansen:** Does NOT work for Shinkansen tickets (use SmartEx instead)
- **Daily Usage:** Expect ¥1,000-1,500 ($7-10) per day for local transport
- **Refund:** If balance left at end of trip, can keep card as souvenir OR refund at JR station (¥220 fee)

##  2. SmartEx (Shinkansen)

**Why:** Buy bullet train tickets on your phone and link them to your Suica. No paper tickets needed!
**App:** "SmartEx" (JR Central).
**Cost Savings:** EX Early Discount: ~¥1,000 ($7) cheaper per ticket if booked 3+ days ahead.

**⚠️ CRITICAL WARNING Regarding 2FA:**
Many Japanese apps (SmartEx, Disney, USJ, Taxi Go) require **SMS Verification** to set up.
Detailed setup MUST be done **at home in the US** while you still have your primary SIM active.
Once you land and switch to an eSIM data-only plan, **you will NOT receive SMS texts** to verify these accounts.
*Do not wait until you land to create these accounts.*

### Setup Steps:
1. Download **SmartEx App** ([iOS](https://apps.apple.com/jp/app/ex/id408357049) | [Android](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=jp.co.jr_central.ExpressPrediction)).
2. Register an account (need email + password).
3. **Link Credit Card:** Add your travel card (Visa/Mastercard/Amex all work).
4. **Link IC Card:**
   - Open Apple Wallet → Tap Suica → Tap 'i' or '...' → Copy **IC Card Number** (17 digits starting with JE...)
   - Paste this number into SmartEx → Link IC Card section
   - *Result:* When you book a Shinkansen, you just tap your phone at the Shinkansen gate. No paper ticket!

### How to Book Tickets:
1. Open SmartEx app → "Reserve Seats"
2. Select: Departure station (e.g., Tokyo) → Arrival (e.g., Kyoto)
3. Choose date and time
4. Select train type: **Nozomi** (fastest) or **Hikari** (stops more)
5. Choose seat: **Window (E seat on Tokyo→Kyoto for Mt. Fuji view!)** or Aisle (D seat)
6. **Smoking:** Always select "Non-Smoking" car
7. Confirm and pay
8. **Done!** Ticket is stored in app and linked to your IC card

### 🚄 Using at Station:
1. Go directly to **Shinkansen gates** (blue gates, separate from regular train gates)
2. Hold phone with Suica against reader (same as regular train)
3. Gate opens - proceed to platform
4. Board your reserved car and seat number
5. **On exit:** Tap phone again at destination Shinkansen gate

### 💡 Pro Tips:
- **Seat Recommendation Tokyo→Kyoto/Osaka:** Get **E seat** (right side of car) for Mt. Fuji views between Tokyo-Shin-Fuji (if clear weather, 7-9 AM best time)
- **Early Booking Discount:** Book 3+ days ahead for EX Early Special (about ¥13,000 vs ¥14,000 Tokyo-Kyoto)
- **Reserved vs Unreserved:** ALWAYS book reserved seat - unreserved cars often packed
- **Luggage:** Regular seats fit carry-on overhead, check larger bags at Yamato counter before boarding
- **EkiBen:** Buy station bento at Tokyo Station before boarding - eat on train!
- **Trip Time:** Tokyo→Kyoto: 2h 15min | Tokyo→Osaka: 2h 45min | Kyoto→Osaka: 30min

##  3. eSIM (Data)

**Why:** Cheaper and faster than carrier roaming. No SIM card swap needed!
**Recommended Providers:** **Ubigi**, **Airalo**, or **Holafly**
**Cost:** ~$20-30 for 10-20GB (plenty for 9 days)

### Setup Steps (1 Week Before Trip):
1. **Check Compatibility:** Settings → General → About → Look for "Digital SIM" or "eSIM"
   - iPhone XS/XR or newer ✅
   - Most newer Android phones ✅
2. Download **Ubigi** ([iOS](https://apps.apple.com/app/ubigi-esim-mobile-internet/id1069080644) | [Android](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.transatel.ubigi)) or **Airalo** app.
3. Create account (email + password).
4. Buy a **Japan Data Plan:**
   - **Ubigi:** 10GB/30 days ~$18
   - **Airalo:** 20GB/30 days ~$26
   - **Holafly:** Unlimited/10 days ~$47 (best for heavy users)
5. **Install eSIM:** Follow app prompts to add eSIM profile to your phone settings.
   - Goes to: Settings → Cellular → Add eSIM → Scan QR code or enter manually
6. **Label It:** Name it "Japan Data" so you know which line to use

### 📶 On Arrival at Airport:
1. **Turn on airplane mode** while still on plane
2. Settings → Cellular → Turn ON "Japan Data" line
3. Settings → Cellular → Turn OFF your primary line's **Data Roaming** (keep primary line on for calls/texts via WiFi)
4. Settings → Cellular → Cellular Data → Select "Japan Data"
5. **Test:** Open Google Maps or Safari - should load immediately
6. **Speed:** Expect 20-50 Mbps (plenty for maps, translation, messaging)

### 💡 Pro Tips:
- **Data Usage:** 10GB is enough for 9 days with maps, translation, and social media
- **Heavy Users:** If streaming video/uploading photos constantly, get 20GB+ or unlimited
- **Hotspot:** Most eSIMs allow hotspot/tethering for traveling companion
- **WhatsApp/iMessage:** Works great on eSIM data
- **Calls:** Use WhatsApp/FaceTime/Discord for calls (free over data). Regular calls cost extra.
- **Battery:** eSIM uses slightly more battery than physical SIM - bring portable charger!
- **Backup:** Download offline maps BEFORE leaving US in case of issues

### 🔧 Troubleshooting:
- **No Service:** Restart phone, takes 1-2 min to connect to network
- **Slow Speed:** Move to open area, sometimes buildings block signal
- **Can't Connect:** Check that airplane mode is OFF and eSIM line is enabled
- **Still Not Working:** Use hotel/cafe WiFi as backup while troubleshooting

##  4. Google Maps

**Why:** Navigation is life. Works better than Apple Maps in Japan.

### Setup Steps (2 Weeks Before Trip):
1. **Download App:** Already have it? Update to latest version.
2. **Offline Maps:** 
   - Open Google Maps → Tap profile picture → Offline maps → Select own map
   - Download **"Tokyo"** area (search Tokyo → Download)
   - Download **"Kyoto"** area
   - Download **"Osaka"** area
   - *Size:* Each map ~500MB-1GB (do on WiFi before trip!)
3. **Save Hotel Locations:** 
   - Search for each hotel → Tap "Save" → Create list "Japan 2026"
   - Advantage: One-tap navigation from anywhere
4. **Star Key Stops:**
   - Akihabara, Senso-ji, Ghibli Museum, Fushimi Inari, Dotonbori, etc.
   - Tap location → Star icon → Shows on map

### 💡 Pro Tips for Using Maps in Japan:
- **Transit Directions:** Shows EXACTLY which car to board for fastest exit (e.g., "Board car 3 for Exit 7")
- **Exit Numbers:** Follow exit numbers religiously - saves 10+ min at complex stations
- **Walking Routes:** More accurate than Apple Maps for pedestrian paths
- **Real-Time Transit:** Shows train delays and cancellations
- **Language:** Works in English, no Japanese needed
- **Pin Hotels:** Drop pin at hotel entrance (not just address) - some buildings have multiple entrances
- **Screenshot Routes:** Take screenshots of key routes in case offline maps fail
- **Battery Saver:** Use offline maps to reduce data/battery usage

### 🚇 Reading Transit Directions:
Google Maps will say: "Take Ginza Line from Shibuya to Asakusa, 23 min, ¥180"
- **What to do:** Follow signs for "Ginza Line" (orange), tap Suica at gate, board train going toward Asakusa direction
- **Transfer:** If map shows transfer, follow "Norikae" (乗り換え) signs at station
- **Platform:** Look for "番線" (Bansen) = Platform Number (e.g., 3番線 = Platform 3)

### 📍 Special Features in Japan:
- **Street View:** Use to preview hotel entrance/restaurant storefront before walking
- **Business Hours:** Shows opening hours, often updated by locals
- **Reviews:** Check Google reviews for restaurants (more reliable than Yelp in Japan)
- **Live Activity:** Shows how busy a location is right now
- **AR Walking:** Point camera to see arrows overlaid on real street (great for confusing intersections)

##  5. Translation

**Why:** Reading menus and signs. Essential for restaurants without English menus.

### Setup Steps:
1. **Google Translate:**
   - Download app: [iOS](https://apps.apple.com/app/google-translate/id414706506) | [Android](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.google.android.apps.translate)
   - Download **Japanese** language pack for offline use
     - Open app → Tap "Japanese" → Download button (arrow)
   - Test the **Camera Tool** (Google Lens) on a Japanese product label at home
2. **DeepL:**
   - Download: [iOS](https://apps.apple.com/app/deepl-translate/id1552407475) | [Android](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.deepl.mobilapp)
   - Better for typing/text translation, more natural phrasing
   - Great for reading long texts or email correspondence

### 📸 How to Use Camera Translation:
1. Open Google Translate → Tap camera icon
2. Point at Japanese text (menu, sign, product label)
3. Tap "Scan" button → Instant English overlay on screen
4. **Pro Mode:** Tap photo button for more accurate translation of complex text

### 💡 Pro Tips:
- **Restaurant Menus:** Take photo of entire menu, translate page by page
- **Allergy Warnings:** Screenshot Japanese translation of your allergen (e.g., "I'm allergic to peanuts") to show staff
- **Useful Phrases:**
  - "This one please" = これをください (Kore o kudasai)
  - "How much?" = いくらですか (Ikura desu ka)
  - "Where is bathroom?" = トイレはどこですか (Toire wa doko desu ka)
- **Conversation Mode:** Real-time voice translation (tap microphone icon) - works okay but not perfect
- **Handwriting:** Draw kanji you see but can't type (handwriting input in Google Translate)
- **Save Translations:** Star frequently used phrases for quick access
- **Offline:** Google Translate works offline after downloading language pack
- **Voice Input:** Speak English → Get Japanese text to show someone

### 🍜 Special: Restaurant Phrases to Save
Pre-translate and save these:
- "I have an allergy to [peanuts/shellfish/dairy]"
- "No [ingredient] please"
- "Check please" (though "Sumimasen" + hand signal works)
- "Is this spicy?"
- "Recommendation?" (though easier to say "Osusume wa?")
- "Is there an English menu?"

##  6. Universal Studios Japan

**Why:** Get Area Timed Entry for Nintendo World. Critical for park success!
**App:** USJ Official App

### Setup Steps (2 Weeks Before Park Day):
1. Download **USJ Official App**: [iOS](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/universal-studios-japan/id532097000) | [Android](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.universalstudios.japanresort)
2. **Register Account:** Email + password
3. **Change Language:** Tap profile → Settings → English
4. **Register Ticket:** 
   - When you receive your QR code tickets (email from Klook or USJ)
   - Tap "Tickets" in app → "Register" → Scan QR code
   - **Do this BEFORE park day** to save time at gate
5. **Practice:** 
   - Learn where the **"Area Timed Entry"** button is (main screen, looks like Nintendo logo)
   - You will need to tap this THE SECOND you walk through the gates

### 🎯 How to Use on Park Day:

#### **Opening Strategy (No Express Pass)**
1. **5:30 AM:** Wake up (yes, really!)
2. **7:00 AM:** Arrive at Universal City Station
3. **7:20 AM:** At park gates (they often open 30-60 min early)
4. **THE MOMENT gates open:**
   - Open USJ App → Tap "Area Timed Entry" → Select "Super Nintendo World"
   - Choose earliest time slot available (usually 10:00 AM or later)
   - **Confirmation:** You get a QR code with your entry time
5. **Sprint to Mario Kart ride** while waiting for your entry time
   - Use Single Rider line if available (much faster!)

#### **With Express Pass (Recommended)**
- Your Express Pass includes a specific Nintendo World entry time (e.g., 10:00-11:00 AM)
- Just show up at that time, scan Express Pass QR, walk right in
- No need for Area Timed Entry lottery

### 📱 App Features:
- **Wait Times:** Real-time ride wait times (updates every 5-10 min)
- **Show Times:** Schedule for parades and character meets
- **Restaurant Reservations:** Timed entry for Kinopio's Cafe (book early!)
- **Map:** Interactive park map with your current location
- **Language:** Full English support
- **Power-Up Band:** Link your band to track game progress (if you buy one)

### 💡 Pro Tips:
- **Battery Drain:** App uses GPS constantly - bring portable charger!
- **Screenshots:** Take screenshots of your QR codes (tickets, Area Entry) in case app crashes
- **Mobile Order:** Use app to order food at select restaurants (skip lines)
- **Single Rider:** Check app for Single Rider availability (Mario Kart, Harry Potter often have it)
- **Low Battery Mode:** If phone dying, turn on Low Power Mode but keep app open
- **Notifications:** Enable notifications for show times and parade schedules

### 🚨 Troubleshooting:
- **App Won't Load:** Use park WiFi "UNIVERSAL_WiFi" (free, no password)
- **Ticket Won't Scan:** Show digital ticket from email instead
- **Lost Connection:** App caches some data, wait times still work offline briefly
- **Account Issues:** Go to Guest Services near park entrance (English staff available)

### ⚡ Power Adapter Details

**Good News:** Japan uses **Type A plugs** (same as US 🇺🇸) and **100V**
- **US Travelers:** NO adapter needed! Your US plugs fit directly.
- **Voltage:** Japan is 100V vs US 110-120V (close enough for all modern devices)
- **Check Device:** If it says "100-240V" on the charger, you're good!

**What You WILL Need:**
- **Multi-port USB charger** - Hotels often have limited outlets
- **Power strip** (optional) - If you have lots of devices

**Voltage Sensitive Devices:**
- Hair dryers, curling irons: Bring dual-voltage (110-240V) OR buy in Japan
- Electric shavers: Usually fine (check label)
- Laptops/phones/cameras: All modern devices handle 100-240V automatically
